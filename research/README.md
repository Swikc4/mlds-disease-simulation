# Research and sources

We already have the following starting sources from our project proposal. We will read and check the relevant sources as we reach each disease; nobody needs to read them all in Week 1.

## Measles
- [Review of measles reproduction numbers](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6302597/) — reported R0 estimates and their variation.
- [CDC measles clinical overview](https://www.cdc.gov/measles/hcp/clinical-overview/index.html) — clinical course, timing, and contagious period.

## Influenza
- [Influenza reproduction-number review](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/25291) — estimates of R0.
- [CDC Yellow Book: Influenza](https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/travel-associated-infections-diseases/influenza.html) — incubation and infectious period.

## COVID-19
- [Review of COVID-19 R0 estimates](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8827628/) — estimates across studies.
- [CDC clinical presentation](https://www.cdc.gov/covid/hcp/clinical-care/covid19-presentation.html) — clinical and timing information.
- [COVID-19 transmission-timing research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10541713/) — incubation, generation and serial intervals.

## Optional historical data
- [Johns Hopkins CSSE COVID-19 repository](https://github.com/CSSEGISandData/COVID-19) — archived historical surveillance data; we need to check whether our intended NYC geographic detail is available before using it.

## How we'll use these sources
When we start adding disease parameters, we'll keep a small table showing the disease, parameter, value, units, source, and label:

- **SOURCED**: stated directly in a source.
- **DERIVED**: calculated from a sourced value, with our calculation explained.
- **ASSUMPTION**: chosen for our simplified model, not a measured disease fact.

These links are from our proposal; they are not yet our completed research or verified model settings. For now, we're planning and learning the basic SIR model.
